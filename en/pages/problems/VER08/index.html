VER08Reliability & Verification

When AI can no longer continue safely, can the system stop instead of forcing an answer?

Agents often optimize for task completion, so without explicit fallback behavior they may continue through failed tools, missing evidence, or unsafe states.

01

What's the problem?

Agents often optimize for task completion, so without explicit fallback behavior they may continue through failed tools, missing evidence, or unsafe states.

02

What do we need to know?

Use verification failures, tool status, workflow state, policy constraints, fallback options, previous stable versions, and compensating actions.

Key inputs

Failure/verification signals, tool status, workflow state, policy constraints, fallback options, previous stable versions, compensating actions

Existing systems

Agent platform, workflow, policy engine, monitoring, queue/ticketing

03

What does AI change?

Safe systems need designed degradation paths: abstain, switch to a deterministic fallback, preserve state, or escalate rather than improvising.

04

How do we solve it?

Define failure modes and the permitted degraded behavior for each one, including rollback, fallback, state preservation, and compensating action.

Solution Core

Graceful Degradation + Abstain + Deterministic Fallback + State Preservation + Compensating Actions

05

What does AI do?

Recognize failed preconditions and choose only among approved fallback paths.

06

What do people decide?

Set fallback priorities, emergency overrides, and recovery responsibility.

07

What improves?

Make failure predictable and containable instead of turning incomplete execution into larger operational damage.
