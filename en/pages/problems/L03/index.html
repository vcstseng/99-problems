L03Legal / Compliance

The control is documented. Is the business actually following it?

A control can exist on paper while operational behavior quietly bypasses it, especially when evidence is spread across systems, approvals, emails, and attachments.

01

What's the problem?

A control can exist on paper while operational behavior quietly bypasses it, especially when evidence is spread across systems, approvals, emails, and attachments.

02

What do we need to know?

Connect control requirements with transactions, system logs, approvals, tickets, documents, exceptions, audit findings, and remediation status.

Key inputs

Control requirements, transactions, system logs, approval emails/tickets, attachments, exceptions, audit findings, remediation

Existing systems

GRC, ERP, workflow/identity, DMS, audit systems

03

What does AI change?

AI helps match messy evidence to control requirements and triage exceptions, while deterministic controls should test what can be tested directly.

04

How do we solve it?

Run deterministic control tests where possible, use AI to interpret unstructured evidence, and track exceptions through remediation.

Solution Core

Deterministic Control Testing + Unstructured Evidence Matching + Exception Triage + Remediation Tracking

05

What does AI do?

Link evidence to control requirements, identify missing or contradictory evidence, and prioritize exceptions.

06

What do people decide?

Judge ambiguous evidence, approve exceptions, and own remediation.

07

What improves?

Detect failing controls earlier and reduce the gap between documented policy and actual operations.
