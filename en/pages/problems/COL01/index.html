COL01Human × AI Collaboration

After adopting AI, which work should go to AI, which stays with people, and how should the workflow change?

Companies often bolt AI onto existing workflows without redesigning task allocation, handoffs, or exceptions, so the new tool adds activity without creating much value.

01

What's the problem?

Companies often bolt AI onto existing workflows without redesigning task allocation, handoffs, or exceptions, so the new tool adds activity without creating much value.

02

What do we need to know?

Break real work into tasks: frequency, effort, complexity, error cost, risk, handoffs, cycle time, current AI use, and employee feedback.

Key inputs

Process maps, task inventory, frequency, complexity, risk, error cost, AI usage, cycle time, handoffs, employee feedback

Existing systems

Workflow, ERP, CRM, process mining, HRIS

03

What does AI change?

AI capability changes quickly, so static job descriptions are a poor unit of design. The better unit is the task and the decision around it.

04

How do we solve it?

Decompose jobs into tasks, decide which should be automated, AI-assisted, or human-owned, then redesign sequence, handoffs, exceptions, and accountability.

Solution Core

Human-AI Work Redesign + Task Allocation

05

What does AI do?

Assess task characteristics and support redesign of execution and evidence flow.

06

What do people decide?

Define business outcomes, non-delegable judgment, and the boundaries of responsibility.

07

What improves?

Turn AI adoption into real workflow improvement rather than another layer of tools.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
