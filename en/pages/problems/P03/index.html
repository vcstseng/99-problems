P03Procurement

With several suppliers, who should get the order—and how much?

Splitting volume across suppliers must balance price, capacity, lead time, quality, resilience, and commercial constraints.

01

What's the problem?

Splitting volume across suppliers must balance price, capacity, lead time, quality, resilience, and commercial constraints.

02

What do we need to know?

Combine demand and BOM requirements with supplier price, MOQ, lead-time variability, capacity, quality, delivery performance, and historical fulfillment.

Key inputs

Demand, BOM, supplier price, MOQ, lead time and variability, capacity, quality, delivery performance, historical fulfillment

Existing systems

ERP/MRP, SRM, QMS, supplier scorecards

03

What does AI change?

Predictive models can improve estimates of supplier performance, while final allocation should be solved under explicit optimization constraints.

04

How do we solve it?

Estimate supplier risk and performance, then optimize volume allocation under capacity, MOQ, cost, resilience, and policy constraints.

Solution Core

Supplier Performance Prediction + Risk-Aware Multi-Supplier Optimization + Scenario Analysis

05

What does AI do?

Update supplier performance expectations and compare allocation scenarios under changing demand or risk.

06

What do people decide?

Set sourcing strategy, diversification requirements, and acceptable trade-offs.

07

What improves?

Allocate orders with a better balance of cost, service, and supply resilience.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
