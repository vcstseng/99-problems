O04Operations

Rush orders, delays, and last-minute changes keep coming. How can the schedule keep up?

Static schedules break when durations, priorities, resources, and dependencies change throughout the day.

01

What's the problem?

Static schedules break when durations, priorities, resources, and dependencies change throughout the day.

02

What do we need to know?

Combine tasks, deadlines, skills, equipment, dependencies, actual duration history, interruptions, notes, and current resource availability.

Key inputs

Work orders/tasks, actual duration history, deadlines, skills, equipment, dependencies, rush/interruption history, notes, available resources

Existing systems

WFM, MES, PPM, FSM/scheduling

03

What does AI change?

AI can predict duration and extract constraints from messy operational inputs, while scheduling itself should respect explicit optimization rules.

04

How do we solve it?

Continuously update durations and constraints, then re-optimize the schedule when meaningful disruptions occur rather than rebuilding it manually.

Solution Core

Duration & Disruption Prediction + Constraint Extraction + Dynamic Scheduling Optimization

05

What does AI do?

Predict duration and disruption risk, extract changing constraints, and trigger better schedule alternatives.

06

What do people decide?

Set priorities, approve high-impact trade-offs, and handle constraints the system cannot represent.

07

What improves?

Keep schedules usable under real-world disruption and reduce avoidable delays and manual replanning.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
