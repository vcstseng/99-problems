O01Operations

Will a new location create new business—or just steal from nearby stores?

A new site can look attractive on local demand while largely cannibalizing existing locations, making projected growth misleading.

01

What's the problem?

A new site can look attractive on local demand while largely cannibalizing existing locations, making projected growth misleading.

02

What do we need to know?

Combine existing-store performance, ramp-up history, customer geography, footfall, demographics, rent, competitors, trade areas, and member transactions.

Key inputs

Existing-site performance, ramp-up, customer geography, footfall, demographics, rent, competitors, trade area, member transactions

Existing systems

POS, CRM/CDP, GIS, ERP, external geo/demographic data

03

What does AI change?

Spatial and demand models can estimate site potential and cannibalization, but scenario assumptions should remain visible to decision makers.

04

How do we solve it?

Forecast the new site's ramp, model overlap with nearby stores, and compare scenarios for net network growth rather than site revenue alone.

Solution Core

New Store Ramp-up Forecasting + Cannibalization Modeling + Spatial Demand Analysis

05

What does AI do?

Estimate local demand, trade-area overlap, and likely transfer of customers from existing locations.

06

What do people decide?

Judge strategic fit, real-estate considerations, and whether the net economics justify opening.

07

What improves?

Avoid approving sites that look strong individually but weaken the network overall.
