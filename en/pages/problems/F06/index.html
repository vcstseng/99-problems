F06Finance

When the market does something we have never seen, what should we stress-test?

Historical scenarios may not cover novel combinations of market, policy, geopolitical, or operational shocks.

01

What's the problem?

Historical scenarios may not cover novel combinations of market, policy, geopolitical, or operational shocks.

02

What do we need to know?

Map exposures to risk factors, review historical analogues, external events, policy changes, and existing stress scenarios, then identify what is genuinely new.

Key inputs

FX/rate/commodity exposures, positions, risk factors, historical events and reactions, external news/policy, existing stress scenarios

Existing systems

TMS/risk system, ERP, market data feeds, external information

03

What does AI change?

AI is useful for connecting emerging events to relevant exposures and finding analogues, but quantitative stress calculations should use governed risk models.

04

How do we solve it?

Translate emerging events into explicit risk-factor shocks, retrieve comparable historical episodes, and feed those scenarios into existing quantitative stress-testing processes.

Solution Core

Event-to-Risk-Factor Mapping + Historical Analogue Retrieval + Existing Quant Stress Testing

05

What does AI do?

Scan external developments, map them to exposures, and propose plausible scenario components and historical analogues.

06

What do people decide?

Decide which scenarios are credible and severe enough to test and approve the final stress assumptions.

07

What improves?

Expand stress testing beyond familiar templates without replacing disciplined risk-model execution.
