DEV01AI-Native Development

AI codes fast. When requirements are vague, is it silently making the decisions for us?

Fast implementation lets unstated assumptions turn into code before anyone realizes the requirement was never actually decided.

01

What's the problem?

Fast implementation lets unstated assumptions turn into code before anyone realizes the requirement was never actually decided.

02

What do we need to know?

Keep requirements, user stories, business rules, acceptance criteria, architecture decisions, assumptions, code, tests, and change history connected.

Key inputs

Requirements, user stories, business rules, acceptance criteria, architecture decisions, assumption register, code, tests, change history

Existing systems

Jira, Confluence, Git, ADR/product docs

03

What does AI change?

AI can detect ambiguity and missing cases, but it can also fill those gaps on its own. The control is to surface assumptions and turn important intent into executable specifications.

04

How do we solve it?

Before coding, force ambiguous assumptions into the open. Convert what can be formalized into acceptance criteria, BDD, contracts, or property tests, then keep checking code against them.

Solution Core

Assumption Extraction + Executable Specification + Acceptance Criteria + Spec–Code Alignment

05

What does AI do?

Identify ambiguity, conflicts, edge cases, and unstated assumptions before implementation.

06

What do people decide?

Define business intent, approve assumptions, and own intentional scope or design changes.

07

What improves?

Reduce hidden intent drift and make important requirements testable before they disappear into code.
