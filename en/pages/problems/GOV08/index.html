GOV08Governance

When AI makes a mistake, who is actually accountable—and can that person really stop it?

Named accountability is meaningless when the designated owner lacks visibility, authority, or practical controls to intervene in the AI system.

01

What's the problem?

Named accountability is meaningless when the designated owner lacks visibility, authority, or practical controls to intervene in the AI system.

02

What do we need to know?

Map organization structure, AI inventory, decision types, process ownership, authority policy, intervention controls, incidents, and audit history.

Key inputs

Org structure, AI/agent inventory, decision types, process/system ownership, authority policies, intervention controls, incident history, audit records

Existing systems

HRIS, CMDB, GRC, agent platform, workflow

03

What does AI change?

AI changes the speed and scale of delegated decisions, making responsibility gaps more consequential. Accountability must be paired with real intervention rights.

04

How do we solve it?

Map every material AI decision to an accountable owner and verify that the owner has the information and controls needed to stop, override, or escalate it.

Solution Core

Accountability Mapping + Decision Ownership + Intervention Authority + Escalation Responsibility

05

What does AI do?

Expose decision ownership, intervention status, and unresolved accountability gaps.

06

What do people decide?

Accept decision ownership and exercise the authority to intervene when needed.

07

What improves?

Replace nominal ownership with accountability that can actually change system behavior.
