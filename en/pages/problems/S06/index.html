S06Sales

How much extra did this promotion actually sell?

Sales usually rise during promotions, but part of that volume may have happened anyway, shifted from another product, or simply been pulled forward.

01

What's the problem?

Sales usually rise during promotions, but part of that volume may have happened anyway, shifted from another product, or simply been pulled forward.

02

What do we need to know?

Compare campaign exposure, discounts, audience, traffic, conversion, margin, inventory, and pre/post sales. Where possible, use holdouts or comparable untreated groups to estimate what would have happened without the promotion.

Key inputs

Campaign, SKU, discount, audience/exposure, traffic, conversion, margin, inventory, pre/post sales

Existing systems

E-commerce, ad platforms, CRM/CDP, ERP, WMS, web analytics

03

What does AI change?

Uplift and causal methods can estimate incremental effect more credibly than simple before/after comparison, especially when promotion behavior varies across customers and products.

04

How do we solve it?

Estimate true incremental lift, account for cannibalization and pull-forward, then evaluate whether the promotion created profitable demand rather than just more transactions.

Solution Core

Promotion Incrementality / Uplift Modeling + Cannibalization Analysis + Constrained Optimization

05

What does AI do?

Estimate incremental sales by segment or SKU and detect where uplift is offset by cannibalization or margin loss.

06

What do people decide?

Choose the comparison design, validate business assumptions, and decide whether the promotion should be repeated or changed.

07

What improves?

Know which promotions create real profitable lift and stop rewarding activity that only looks successful.
