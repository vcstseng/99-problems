H03HR

What should we pay without falling behind the market or breaking internal equity?

Compensation decisions must balance external market rates, internal job value, equity, performance, and budget at the same time.

01

What's the problem?

Compensation decisions must balance external market rates, internal job value, equity, performance, and budget at the same time.

02

What do we need to know?

Normalize external salary benchmarks against internal job architecture, responsibilities, skills, levels, pay, performance, and budget.

Key inputs

External salary benchmarks, job descriptions, responsibilities, grades, skills, internal pay, performance, budget

Existing systems

HRIS, compensation system, salary surveys, job architecture, EPM

03

What does AI change?

AI can improve semantic job matching across inconsistent titles and support pay-equity analysis, while policy and compensation decisions remain human-owned.

04

How do we solve it?

Map internal roles to credible external comparators, analyze internal pay distribution, and present market and equity trade-offs together.

Solution Core

Semantic Job Matching + Compensation Benchmarking + Pay Equity Analysis

05

What does AI do?

Match jobs semantically, benchmark market ranges, and flag unexplained pay differences or outliers.

06

What do people decide?

Set compensation philosophy, acceptable ranges, equity principles, and final pay decisions.

07

What improves?

Make pay decisions that are more competitive, internally coherent, and explainable.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
