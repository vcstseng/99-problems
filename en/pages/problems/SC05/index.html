SC05Supply Chain

Order volume changes every day. How many people does the distribution center need each hour?

Daily and intraday workload variability makes fixed staffing plans either expensive or insufficient.

01

What's the problem?

Daily and intraday workload variability makes fixed staffing plans either expensive or insufficient.

02

What do we need to know?

Use inbound and outbound volume, SKU mix, cutoff times, processing speed, workstation capacity, skills, shifts, attendance, and labor rules.

Key inputs

Inbound/outbound volume, SKU profile, cutoff, historical processing speed, workstation capacity, skills, shifts, attendance, labor rules

Existing systems

WMS, WFM, ERP

03

What does AI change?

AI can improve hourly workload forecasts, while staffing optimization should respect labor and capacity constraints explicitly.

04

How do we solve it?

Forecast work by time bucket, convert demand into labor requirements, and optimize shifts under skill, labor-rule, and workstation constraints.

Solution Core

Hourly Workload Forecasting + Capacity Prediction + Constraint-Based Workforce Scheduling

05

What does AI do?

Predict workload and processing demand by hour and identify likely capacity gaps.

06

What do people decide?

Set service priorities and approve staffing trade-offs, overtime, and contingency plans.

07

What improves?

Match labor more closely to actual workload and reduce both overtime and service failures.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
