CS02Customer Service

A complaint just arrived. How urgent is it, who should own it, and should it escalate now?

Manual triage is slow and inconsistent, while simple keyword rules miss context such as customer importance, product risk, SLA, and severity.

01

What's the problem?

Manual triage is slow and inconsistent, while simple keyword rules miss context such as customer importance, product risk, SLA, and severity.

02

What do we need to know?

Use the conversation, customer and product context, historical cases, SLA, risk type, queue state, and agent skills.

Key inputs

Ticket/conversation text, customer and product context, historical cases, SLA, risk type, queue, agent skills

Existing systems

Ticketing/CCaaS, CRM, KB, WFM

03

What does AI change?

Language models can classify intent and risk from unstructured complaints, while SLA and routing eligibility should remain explicit.

04

How do we solve it?

Classify the issue, estimate urgency and risk, apply SLA rules, then route or escalate to the right queue with clear evidence.

Solution Core

Intent & Risk Classification + Urgency Detection + SLA-Aware Routing + Escalation

05

What does AI do?

Understand the complaint, detect urgency and risk, and recommend routing or escalation.

06

What do people decide?

Handle ambiguous or high-impact cases and define routing and escalation policy.

07

What improves?

Get urgent complaints to the right people sooner and reduce avoidable SLA misses.

08

Experiment: risk before customer value

Bounded policy-triage and process-transfer checks passed. A standard customer’s preclassified critical-risk ticket is placed before routine VIP billing. Suggestions require qualified, available staff; missing evidence or uncertain signals trigger evidence requests or human review.

Synthetic main case

T-critical: P0, incident queue, qualified incident-1; response deadline 11:45 UTC, already past at the fixed 12:00 UTC comparison time.
T-outage: P1, technical queue, tech-1; deadline 12:30 UTC.
T-vip: P2, billing queue, billing-1; deadline 15:45 UTC.

All dates are 2026-10-26. SLA values and signals are synthetic policy inputs, not industry standards or customer promises. These are recommendations, not dispatch actions.

What failed before the changes?

The actual pre-change probe found that the process hardcoded the previous domain’s analyst A12 instead of the frozen CS02 assignment A05. The existing customer-service worker could retrieve answers but could not triage. The failure was retained; analyst configuration and bounded policy tools were added without changing the seven gates or relaxing the acceptance criteria.

09

Reuse the process; keep human decisions explicit

The same seven gates now use A05 for service analysis. Reviewer, verifier and simulated-manager gates remain protected. Unknown categories or risks, unavailable staff, expired policy and malformed timestamps cannot be silently treated as a normal success.

Evidence and checks

Eight synthetic scenarios; 27 new checks and 276 total regression checks passed. The E93 regression was retained. Separate checks reject invented priorities, deadlines, assignees or execution claims; unresolved cases cannot be accepted, and stale decisions or changed sources are blocked.

Experiment, original failure and limits · Synthetic policy · Structured tickets and roster · Frozen protocol

Deterministic rules use preclassified fields; this does not test arbitrary complaint-text understanding, isolated models, workload optimization or real SLA improvement. Reviews are simulated by the same assistant. No ticket was assigned, no message sent and no customer commitment made.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Current scope: Policy-triage demo · synthetic structured tickets
Bounded priority, queue and escalation recommendations passed; live dispatch and general language understanding remain unvalidated.
