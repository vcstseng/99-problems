SC06Supply Chain

After shipment, which load is most likely to be late—and should we intervene now?

Late-delivery risk changes after dispatch as carrier performance, route events, weather, traffic, and network conditions evolve.

01

What's the problem?

Late-delivery risk changes after dispatch as carrier performance, route events, weather, traffic, and network conditions evolve.

02

What do we need to know?

Connect shipment details with destination, carrier, rate, capacity, on-time history, tracking events, route, traffic, weather, and historical delays.

Key inputs

Shipment, destination, carrier, rate, capacity/OTD, tracking events, traffic, weather, route, historical delays

Existing systems

TMS, WMS, carrier APIs, maps/external data

03

What does AI change?

AI can predict delay risk from evolving event streams, while routing and carrier choices should respect cost, contract, and operational constraints.

04

How do we solve it?

Continuously update ETA and delay risk, flag shipments where early intervention still matters, and compare reroute or carrier alternatives.

Solution Core

ETA / Delay Risk Prediction + Shipment Exception Detection + Routing/Carrier Re-Optimization

05

What does AI do?

Detect exception patterns, predict late-arrival risk, and recommend intervention options.

06

What do people decide?

Decide when the cost of intervention is justified and choose customer or logistics actions.

07

What improves?

Intervene earlier on shipments that are truly at risk and reduce avoidable late deliveries.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
