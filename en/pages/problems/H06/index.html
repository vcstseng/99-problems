H06HR

Which teams or critical roles are seeing rising attrition risk—and why?

Attrition risk becomes dangerous when it concentrates in critical teams or capabilities, yet individual prediction can be noisy and ethically sensitive.

01

What's the problem?

Attrition risk becomes dangerous when it concentrates in critical teams or capabilities, yet individual prediction can be noisy and ethically sensitive.

02

What do we need to know?

Look at tenure, role movement, compensation, performance, engagement, team structure, workload, and historical attrition primarily at segment and workforce-risk level.

Key inputs

Tenure, role changes, compensation, performance, engagement, team structure, workload, historical attrition

Existing systems

HRIS, survey platforms, performance systems, organization data

03

What does AI change?

AI can detect changing workforce-risk patterns and contributing drivers, but should not be treated as a deterministic prediction of an individual's intent to leave.

04

How do we solve it?

Monitor risk by team, role, and capability, identify the drivers that are moving, and focus interventions where business exposure is highest.

Solution Core

Workforce Risk Analytics + Segment-Level Attrition Modeling + Driver Analysis

05

What does AI do?

Detect segments with rising attrition patterns and rank the observable factors associated with that change.

06

What do people decide?

Interpret local context, choose retention or workforce actions, and avoid punitive use of individual risk scores.

07

What improves?

Spot workforce risk earlier while keeping decisions focused on organizational action rather than surveillance.
