S05Sales

What price balances volume, margin, and inventory?

Pricing decisions affect demand, margin, and stock at the same time, so a price that looks good on one metric can hurt the business elsewhere.

01

What's the problem?

Pricing decisions affect demand, margin, and stock at the same time, so a price that looks good on one metric can hurt the business elsewhere.

02

What do we need to know?

Use historical price changes, demand, promotions, margins, inventory, competitor prices, seasonality, and any controlled pricing experiments. The key is separating correlation from the demand effect of price itself.

Key inputs

Historical prices, volume, promotions, margins, inventory, competitor prices, seasonality, price-change or experiment history

Existing systems

ERP, POS/e-commerce, CRM, WMS, DWH/BI, external price data

03

What does AI change?

AI can model complex demand patterns, but pricing decisions still need causal evidence and explicit business constraints such as margin floors, stock levels, and channel rules.

04

How do we solve it?

Estimate price elasticity where evidence supports it, model demand under alternative prices, and optimize within commercial and inventory constraints.

Solution Core

Causal Price Elasticity + Demand Modeling + Constrained Optimization

05

What does AI do?

Estimate demand response, compare price scenarios, and identify options that balance revenue, margin, and stock exposure.

06

What do people decide?

Set pricing guardrails, strategic objectives, and acceptable trade-offs across volume, margin, and inventory.

07

What improves?

Make pricing decisions with clearer evidence and fewer avoidable margin or inventory mistakes.
