CS03Customer Service

Across thousands of support conversations, where is service quality getting worse?

Sampling a small share of conversations misses emerging quality issues and inconsistent behavior across channels and agents.

01

What's the problem?

Sampling a small share of conversations misses emerging quality issues and inconsistent behavior across channels and agents.

02

What do we need to know?

Use calls, chats, emails, tickets, the QA rubric, policies, human QA results, CSAT, and service standards to evaluate interactions against the same expectations.

Key inputs

Calls/chats/emails, tickets, QA rubric, policies/processes, human QA, CSAT, service standards

Existing systems

CCaaS, ticketing, CRM, transcripts, QA systems

03

What does AI change?

AI can evaluate large conversation volumes against a rubric, but calibration with human reviewers is essential because the evaluator can drift or misread context.

04

How do we solve it?

Apply rubric-grounded review across the full interaction set, surface quality-risk patterns, and send uncertain or high-impact cases to human calibration.

Solution Core

Rubric-Grounded Conversation Evaluation + Quality Risk Detection + Human Calibration

05

What does AI do?

Score conversations against explicit criteria, detect recurring quality issues, and prioritize interactions for review.

06

What do people decide?

Define the rubric, calibrate judgments, and decide coaching or policy changes.

07

What improves?

See service-quality deterioration earlier and focus human QA on the cases that need judgment.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
