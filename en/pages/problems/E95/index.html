E95System Effects

If AI predictions change people's actions, can the later 'evidence' simply be an effect of the model itself?

Prediction changes resource allocation and behavior, which changes outcomes and future training data, creating a loop where the model learns from the world it helped create.

01

What's the problem?

Prediction changes resource allocation and behavior, which changes outcomes and future training data, creating a loop where the model learns from the world it helped create.

02

What do we need to know?

Track predictions, downstream actions, exposure or treatment, outcomes, training data, and model versions so natural outcomes can be separated from intervention effects.

Key inputs

Predictions, scores, downstream actions, resource allocation, exposure/treatment, outcomes, training data, model versions

Existing systems

CRM, recommendation engine, decision system, experiment platform, ML logs

03

What does AI change?

Once predictions influence treatment, observational outcomes are no longer neutral evidence. Causal methods and deliberate exploration are needed to understand what would have happened otherwise.

04

How do we solve it?

Track feedback loops, preserve holdouts or exploration where appropriate, and use counterfactual or causal evaluation to test whether the model is validating itself.

Solution Core

Feedback-Loop Detection + Exploration Policy + Counterfactual / Causal Evaluation

05

What does AI do?

Detect links between predictions, actions, and subsequent data and surface signs of self-reinforcing behavior.

06

What do people decide?

Decide where exploration must be preserved and when the organization should deliberately break the loop.

07

What improves?

Reduce the risk that AI mistakes the consequences of its own intervention for proof that its original prediction was correct.
