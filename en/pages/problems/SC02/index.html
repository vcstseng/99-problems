SC02Supply Chain

Customer forecasts keep moving. How much long-lead material and capacity should we commit?

Customer forecasts are biased and volatile, but long lead times force material and capacity commitments before firm orders arrive.

01

What's the problem?

Customer forecasts are biased and volatile, but long lead times force material and capacity commitments before firm orders arrive.

02

What do we need to know?

Compare forecast history with actual orders and pull-through, BOM requirements, inventory, lead time, MOQ, and capacity to understand both bias and uncertainty.

Key inputs

Customer forecasts, actual orders/pull, forecast accuracy and bias, BOM, inventory, lead time, MOQ, capacity

Existing systems

ERP/MRP, APS, S&OP, WMS

03

What does AI change?

Probabilistic models can correct recurring forecast bias and quantify uncertainty, while planning decisions must still honor physical and commercial constraints.

04

How do we solve it?

Calibrate customer forecasts against historical behavior, generate demand ranges, and test material and capacity commitments under multiple scenarios.

Solution Core

Customer Forecast Bias Correction + Probabilistic Demand Modeling + Material & Capacity Scenario Planning

05

What does AI do?

Estimate forecast bias, produce demand distributions, and show the risk of alternative commitment levels.

06

What do people decide?

Set service and inventory risk tolerance and approve material and capacity commitments.

07

What improves?

Reduce both excess commitments and shortages caused by treating customer forecasts as certain.

How does the agent framework approach this problem?

Start with the decision and evidence needs, then combine consulting skills and approved tools under human review, security and governance. The framework defines the approach; this page alone does not establish implemented capability or proven results for this problem.
