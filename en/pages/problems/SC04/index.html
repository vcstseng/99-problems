SC04Supply Chain

One location cannot sell it while another is out of stock. Is demand weak—or is inventory in the wrong place?

Observed sales understate true demand when stores stock out, while excess stock elsewhere can make the network look balanced when it is not.

01

What's the problem?

Observed sales understate true demand when stores stock out, while excess stock elsewhere can make the network look balanced when it is not.

02

What do we need to know?

Use SKU-by-location sales, stockouts, on-hand and in-transit inventory, lost-sales signals, lead times, promotions, and service levels.

Key inputs

SKU × location sales, stockout/OOS history, on-hand/in-transit inventory, lost-sales signals, lead time, promotions, service level

Existing systems

ERP, WMS, POS, TMS

03

What does AI change?

Demand models can estimate censored demand where stockouts suppress observed sales, enabling better rebalancing decisions.

04

How do we solve it?

Estimate lost demand, distinguish weak demand from stock unavailability, and recommend rebalancing across locations before ordering more.

Solution Core

Censored Demand Estimation + Stockout/Lost-Sales Modeling + Inventory Rebalancing

05

What does AI do?

Infer unmet demand, identify mismatched inventory placement, and compare rebalance options.

06

What do people decide?

Set service targets, logistics constraints, and commercial priorities.

07

What improves?

Reduce simultaneous overstock and stockout by putting inventory where demand actually is.
