VER05Reliability & Verification

If another AI is doing the grading, who verifies the judge?

AI evaluators can be biased, inconsistent, or share the same blind spots as the system they score, creating false confidence in quality metrics.

01

What's the problem?

AI evaluators can be biased, inconsistent, or share the same blind spots as the system they score, creating false confidence in quality metrics.

02

What do we need to know?

Compare judge scores with rubrics, human labels, gold sets, deterministic checks, disagreement cases, and evaluator versions.

Key inputs

Model outputs, judge scores, rubrics, human labels, gold sets, deterministic test results, disagreement cases, judge versions

Existing systems

Evaluation platform, annotation tools, CI/evaluation store

03

What does AI change?

LLM-as-judge can scale evaluation, but it must itself be calibrated and tested against independent evidence.

04

How do we solve it?

Validate evaluators on gold sets, test systematic bias and disagreement, and combine AI judgment with deterministic checks and targeted human review.

Solution Core

Evaluator Calibration + Gold-Set Validation + Bias Testing + Independent Checks

05

What does AI do?

Score large evaluation sets and surface disagreement patterns for calibration.

06

What do people decide?

Define the rubric, maintain gold examples, and decide when evaluator performance is good enough for use.

07

What improves?

Keep automated evaluation from becoming an unverified source of truth.
