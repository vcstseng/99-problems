O03Operations

How much work will arrive tomorrow or next week, and can capacity handle it?

Short-horizon workload changes faster than staffing and equipment plans, causing queues, overtime, missed SLAs, or idle capacity.

01

What's the problem?

Short-horizon workload changes faster than staffing and equipment plans, causing queues, overtime, missed SLAs, or idle capacity.

02

What do we need to know?

Use historical arrivals, processing time, demand signals, staffing, equipment, and available capacity at the time granularity where the work must actually be scheduled.

Key inputs

Historical orders/work orders/cases, arrival rate, service/processing time, demand signals, labor, equipment, available capacity

Existing systems

ERP, WMS/MES, WFM, FSM, scheduling systems

03

What does AI change?

AI can improve short-term demand forecasts, while queueing and capacity math should remain explicit.

04

How do we solve it?

Forecast near-term workload, convert it into required capacity, and flag periods where expected demand exceeds available resources.

Solution Core

Short-Horizon Workload Forecasting + Queue/Capacity Modeling + Bottleneck Prediction

05

What does AI do?

Update workload forecasts and predict likely bottlenecks as new demand signals arrive.

06

What do people decide?

Set service priorities and decide staffing, overtime, outsourcing, or capacity changes.

07

What improves?

Reduce last-minute capacity surprises and improve service performance.
