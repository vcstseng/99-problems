GOV05Governance

When AI encounters a situation the rules never covered, how does it know to stop and hand off?

Novel cases can fall outside known policies, yet goal-driven agents may continue anyway and produce confident but unauthorized outcomes.

01

What's the problem?

Novel cases can fall outside known policies, yet goal-driven agents may continue anyway and produce confident but unauthorized outcomes.

02

What do we need to know?

Use policy rules, authority boundaries, required evidence, verifier results, exception history, prior escalations, and execution traces.

Key inputs

Policy rules, authority boundaries, required evidence, source/verifier results, exceptions, historical escalations, agent traces

Existing systems

Agent platform, policy engine, workflow, ticketing

03

What does AI change?

Abstention should be triggered by missing evidence, failed verification, policy gaps, or authority limits—not by vague self-reported uncertainty alone.

04

How do we solve it?

Define explicit escalation conditions, independent verification, abstention rules, and a structured human handoff for uncovered cases.

Solution Core

Rule & Evidence-Based Escalation + Independent Verification + Abstention Policy + Human Handoff

05

What does AI do?

Detect when evidence or authority is insufficient and assemble the context needed for escalation.

06

What do people decide?

Resolve novel cases and update policy when the same exception begins to recur.

07

What improves?

Make stopping a designed behavior rather than a failure mode.
