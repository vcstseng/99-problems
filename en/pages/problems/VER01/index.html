VER01Reliability & Verification

Are AI answers, analyses, and reports actually supported by evidence?

AI can produce fluent claims that cite weak, irrelevant, derived, or nonexistent evidence, making plausibility look like verification.

01

What's the problem?

AI can produce fluent claims that cite weak, irrelevant, derived, or nonexistent evidence, making plausibility look like verification.

02

What do we need to know?

Track the response, every material claim, retrieved sources, citations, source authority, original-versus-derived labels, and data lineage.

Key inputs

AI responses, analysis/report outputs, retrieved sources, citations, source authority, original/derived labels, data lineage

Existing systems

RAG, DMS, search, DWH/BI, data catalog/lineage, LLM logs

03

What does AI change?

RAG alone does not prove that evidence supports the claim. Verification must check source quality, citation correctness, and claim-to-evidence entailment.

04

How do we solve it?

Require important claims to link to authoritative sources, verify that the cited evidence actually supports them, and distinguish primary evidence from AI-derived content.

Solution Core

Source Authority + Citation Verification + Claim–Evidence Entailment + Provenance & Derived-Content Labeling

05

What does AI do?

Map claims to evidence and flag unsupported, weakly supported, or circular citations.

06

What do people decide?

Define authoritative sources and decide what level of evidence is required for high-impact claims.

07

What improves?

Reduce confident unsupported outputs and make evidence quality visible.
